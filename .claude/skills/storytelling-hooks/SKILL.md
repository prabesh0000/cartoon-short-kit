# Storytelling Hooks: the six psychology levers
(Condensed from yaxeen/storytelling-skills, MIT; see `licenses/`. Read `patterns.md` in this folder before writing options.)

Short-form content lives or dies in the first 2 seconds. A strong hook stacks 2-3 levers in one line; a full script runs them in order. **A hook has two jobs: stop the scroll AND filter for the right viewer.** A hook that grabs everyone attracts the wrong audience, they leave at second 3 and retention tanks; a qualifying word costs a little reach and buys retention.

## The six levers
1. **Curiosity gap:** open a question, don't answer it. Reference something specific but unnamed ("this one setting"); add a stake (reward gained or problem removed) for two gaps. *"Most people lose money on this app without noticing the one toggle draining it."*
2. **Emotional mirror:** target the feeling under the fact, so the content becomes about *them*. *Fact: "a tip for staying productive." Mirror: "If your to-do list makes you feel behind before the day starts, this is for you."*
3. **Conflict engine:** a goal plus an obstacle; no obstacle, no tension. The most gripping obstacles are often self-made (a fear or excuse). *"You're doing everything right to grow, and one invisible habit is capping your results."*
4. **Relatability:** the broadest shared feeling *inside the audience you want*, with a qualifying signal kept in the line ("waking up tired" attracts everyone and converts no one; "tired because a client's revisions kept you up" finds freelancers).
5. **Pattern + surprise:** set an expectation, then break it; "but" is the switch, and surprise needs a setup first. *"She followed every rule in the productivity book. But what finally worked wasn't in it."*
6. **Three-act structure:** setup (ordinary world) → problem (tension enters) → resolution (outcome with a lesson or feeling).

## The three hook layers (short-form video, most feeds autoplay muted)
| Layer | Rule |
|---|---|
| Visual first frame (0:00) | Must intrigue with sound off: motion, an odd object, a mid-action moment |
| Spoken line | The first sentence; where the levers live |
| Text overlay | Complements the spoken line, never duplicates it: the other half of the gap (title + thumbnail pairing) |

Always deliver all three layers, not just the spoken line. Flash the key phrase of later beats (twist, payoff) as overlays for muted viewers too.

## Workflow
1. **Intake:** niche, *exact* audience (who should follow, not just watch), platform, goal (follows / saves / sales / views), tone. If key facts are missing ask up to 2 questions when the user can answer, otherwise state assumptions and proceed.
2. **Variants:** read `patterns.md`, write 5+ hook options each stacking 2-3 labelled levers; adapt patterns to the niche, never reuse a worked example verbatim.
3. **Select:** score against the checklist below; recommend one and say why (which gaps it opens, who it qualifies).
4. **Script:** expand the winner in lever order, with all three hook layers specified for the opening.

## Building a full script
Most short scripts are 3-6 lines (~20-45 s; check pace at the voice's real rate). Order: **open** (1 + 4, often + 2: a curiosity gap in a relatable feeling, working in 2 s) → **build tension** (3: obstacle or common mistake, raise stakes) → **twist** if possible (5: break the expectation) → **pay off** (6: the lesson or emotional point) → **bookend**.
- **Bookend + one CTA:** repeat a key phrase or number from the hook in the final line (the brain registers "completion" and the viewer comments or shares). One CTA matched to the goal (follows: "I post [X] for [audience] every week"; saves: "save this for your next [moment]"; comments: one specific question), folded into the bookend line, not tacked on.
- Concrete numbers and time gaps ("3 times", "in 7 days") sharpen a hook; use a specific number whenever facts allow.
- Bad → good: "Here are 3 budgeting tips for freelancers." (a topic label: no gap, feeling or tension) → "You landed a $3,000 client last month, so why is your bank account still empty?" (relatability + mirror + gap + a number, and "client" qualifies).

## Common mistakes
Hook answers its own question (keep the gap open until the payoff) · chases everyone, "stop scrolling!" (add a qualifying word) · only the spoken line written (specify first frame + overlay) · overlay repeats the spoken line · two ideas in one hook · a gap the video never pays off (clickbait kills retention and trust; close every loop) · vague where a number exists.

## Pre-publish checklist
- [ ] Line 1 opens a curiosity gap that works in under 2 s · [ ] a real feeling is targeted, not a dry fact · [ ] goal + obstacle create tension
- [ ] the feeling is universal within the target audience, with a qualifying signal · [ ] all three hook layers specified
- [ ] at least one expectation-breaking moment · [ ] setup → problem → resolution · [ ] the final line bookends the opening
- If a line is flat, ask which lever is missing and add it.
