## Sources (checked 2026-11-26)
- More than 7,000 chemicals in tobacco smoke; at least 69-70 cause cancer: CDC, https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/about/cigarettes-and-cancer.html ; https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/21587/cdc_21587_DS1.pdf ; Health Canada, https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/smoking-tobacco/health-effects-smoking-second-hand-smoke/harmful-chemicals.html
- Vapes don't burn tobacco, no tar or CO; far fewer toxins at lower levels; substantially less harmful; full benefit only if you stop smoking completely: NHS, https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/quit-smoking/vaping-to-quit-smoking/ ; https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/quit-smoking/vaping-to-quit-smoking/vaping-myths-and-the-facts
- Not risk-free; not recommended for non-smokers: NHS, https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/quit-smoking/using-e-cigarettes-to-stop-smoking/ ; ASH, https://ash.org.uk/health-inequalities/vaping-and-tobacco-harm-reduction
- Aerosol can contain nicotine, heavy metals (nickel, tin, lead), ultrafine particles inhaled deep into the lungs: CDC via Missouri DHSS, https://health.mo.gov/living/wellness/tobacco/smokingandtobacco/pdf/vaping-providers.pdf
- Nicotine in adolescence can harm attention, learning, mood, impulse control: CDC quick facts (archived), https://webharvest.gov/congress115th/20190109010957/https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/basic_information/e-cigarettes/Quick-Facts-on-the-Risks-of-E-cigarettes-for-Kids-Teens-and-Young-Adults.html ; US Surgeon General, https://e-cigarettes.surgeongeneral.gov/knowtherisks.html
- (Long version only) Long-term vapers may get roughly similar nicotine to smokers, results variable: Shahab et al., Ann Intern Med 2017, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166548/
